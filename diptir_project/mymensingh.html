River stories & folk traditions

Mymensingh Division

From the Brahmaputra riverbank to the Madhupur forest, Mymensingh is a region of art, agriculture, and living folk memory.

Introduction & location

At the meeting of river and forest

Mymensingh Division lies north of Dhaka and borders India to the north. The old Brahmaputra flows through its broad plains, while the Madhupur Tract adds elevated red-soil land and forest to the region’s varied landscape.

The division’s history is tied to river trade, agriculture, education, and the celebrated folk ballads collected from the wider Mymensingh region.

Brahmaputra Riverfront

The old riverbank is a defining part of Mymensingh city, with open views and a long connection to local trade and everyday life.

Madhupur National Park

Sal forest and red-earth trails offer a different landscape from the surrounding floodplains and agricultural fields.

Netrokona Haor

In the north, wetlands and seasonal waters create striking landscapes and support fishing, farming, and birdlife.

Heritage & everyday life

Folk memory, learning, and a working landscape

Historical importance

The region is celebrated for the Mymensingh Geetika, a collection of Bengali folk ballads that preserves stories of love, courage, and rural life. River trade, colonial-era institutions, and the 1971 Liberation War also shaped its history.

Culture & traditions

Folk music, pala gaan, boat traditions, seasonal fairs, and rural theatre remain important cultural expressions. Mymensingh is also known for its educational institutions and the artistic life around them.

Famous local foods

Mymensingh is especially associated with freshwater fish, including farmed fish from Trishal and surrounding areas. Rice, pitha, dairy sweets, and seasonal produce round out a varied local table.

Economy & natural resources

Agriculture, aquaculture, livestock, education, and small industries are important to the division. Fertile floodplains support crops, while ponds and farms make fish production a major livelihood.

Rivers, forests & biodiversity

The old Brahmaputra, Kangsha, and other waterways connect river plains and haors. Madhupur’s Sal forest and northern wetlands provide habitat for wildlife and valuable natural resources.

Why Mymensingh matters to Bangladesh

Mymensingh contributes to national food and fish production and preserves an important body of Bengali folk literature. Its rivers, wetlands, forests, and learning communities are an essential part of Bangladesh’s shared heritage.
