The green north

Rangpur Division

Open agricultural plains, historic temples, river landscapes, and resilient northern communities.

Introduction & location

Wide plains and living heritage

Rangpur Division covers much of north-western Bangladesh, between the Himalayan foothills and the Teesta–Brahmaputra river system. The landscape is largely agricultural, with floodplains, sandy chars, and important wetlands.

Its history is visible in temples, archaeological remains, and the legacy of the ancient Kamrup region, while its towns are known for education, farming, and northern hospitality.

Discover the region

Temples, tea, and northern landscapes

Kantajew Temple

Dinajpur’s eighteenth-century terracotta temple is celebrated for its intricate brick ornament and Hindu devotional heritage.

Teesta River

Riverbanks and chars reveal a landscape continually reshaped by water, with farming and fishing at its heart.

Panchagarh Tea Gardens

Tea cultivation has brought a new character to Bangladesh’s northernmost district, set against distant Himalayan views on clear days.

Heritage & everyday life

Agriculture and identity in the north

Historical importance

The ancient settlement of Mahasthangarh lies nearby in the wider north-west, while Dinajpur’s temples and estates reflect centuries of regional history. Rangpur also holds an important place in the political and cultural history of northern Bengal.

Culture & traditions

Rural fairs, folk songs, seasonal celebrations, and crafts such as bamboo work enrich local life. The region’s dialects and traditions express a strong northern identity shaped by farming and river communities.

Famous local foods

Dinajpur is famed for its lychees, while northern rice dishes, pitha, and dairy sweets are widely enjoyed. Seasonal produce and locally raised crops are central to the region’s cuisine.

Economy & natural resources

Rice, potatoes, maize, wheat, tea, and other crops make agriculture a major livelihood. Fertile soils and growing tea areas contribute to the economy, alongside trade and regional services.

Rivers, forests & biodiversity

The Teesta, Dharla, and Brahmaputra-Jamuna systems support chars, wetlands, and agricultural floodplains. These changing habitats are important for freshwater fish, migratory birds, and rural livelihoods.

Why Rangpur matters to Bangladesh

Rangpur is a major agricultural region and a keeper of distinctive northern heritage. Its river systems and farming communities are essential to food production and to understanding the country’s relationship with flood and drought.
