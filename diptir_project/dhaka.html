The heart of Bangladesh

Dhaka Division

A meeting place of river life, centuries-old architecture, national history, and the energy of a modern capital.

Introduction & location

Where the country comes together

Dhaka Division sits in central Bangladesh, where the Buriganga and other waterways meet the broad floodplains of the Ganges–Brahmaputra delta. Its namesake city is the national capital and a centre for public life, education, commerce, and the arts.

Beyond the capital’s busy streets, the division includes river islands, fertile farmland, historic settlements, and the old muslin and textile landscapes of Bengal.

Heritage & everyday life

A living capital with deep roots

Historical importance

Dhaka grew as a Mughal provincial capital and later became a major centre of Bengal’s trade and administration. The Language Movement and the events leading to the 1971 Liberation War gave the city a defining place in Bangladesh’s national story.

Culture & traditions

Dhaka brings together traditions from across Bangladesh. New Year celebrations, book fairs, theatre, crafts, religious festivals, and the lively markets of Old Dhaka all shape its distinctive urban culture.

Famous local foods

Dhaka is renowned for kacchi biryani, bakarkhani, kebabs, haleem, and sweets such as shahi jilapi. Its food streets reflect generations of Bengali, Mughal, and regional influences.

Economy & natural resources

Government, finance, education, transport, and the ready-made garment industry are central to the division’s economy. Fertile alluvial soils and waterways also support farming, fisheries, and inland trade.

Rivers, forests & biodiversity

The Buriganga, Turag, Dhaleshwari, and Shitalakshya connect communities and habitats across the delta. Floodplains and wetlands support fish, waterbirds, and seasonal agriculture, while Sal forests remain in parts of Gazipur and nearby areas.

Why Dhaka matters to Bangladesh

As the seat of national institutions and a major engine of jobs, education, and culture, Dhaka connects people and ideas from every region. Protecting its heritage and waterways is important to the future of the entire country.
